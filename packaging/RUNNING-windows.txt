perch — Twitch in one native window
===================================

This zip is the whole app: one executable, no installer, nothing written
outside your own user folder. Unzip it anywhere and run perch.exe.

It needs three things it cannot ship, and it will tell you which one is
missing when you hit it. Here they are up front instead.


1. streamlink — required
------------------------

perch does not talk to Twitch's video servers itself; streamlink does, and
perch plays what it hands over.

    https://streamlink.github.io/install.html

The Windows installer is the easy answer. Anything that puts `streamlink` on
your PATH works, and if yours lives somewhere unusual, set STREAMLINK_PATH to
the full path of the executable.

Without it: a pane opens and says "streamlink not found".


2. libmpv-2.dll — required
--------------------------

The decoder. There is no official Windows package of just this file, which is
the annoying part. It ships inside several media players, and perch looks in
those install folders, beside its own executable, and along PATH.

The simplest fix is to put a copy of libmpv-2.dll in this folder, next to
perch.exe. You can take it from an mpv.net or Plex install, or from the mpv
Windows development builds:

    https://sourceforge.net/projects/mpv-player-windows/files/libmpv/

Set MPV_DLL to a full path if you would rather keep it elsewhere.

Without it: a pane opens and says it could not find libmpv, and lists every
directory it looked in.

Note that perch never searches the *working* directory for this file. That is
deliberate: an app run out of a shared downloads folder would otherwise load
whatever DLL somebody else left there.


3. A Twitch Client ID — required to see who you follow
------------------------------------------------------

perch has no application key baked into it, so you register your own. It takes
a minute and costs nothing:

  - Go to https://dev.twitch.tv/console and register an application.
  - Set OAuth Redirect URL to http://localhost (the form demands one; perch
    never uses it).
  - Set Client Type to Public. There is no client secret to store.
  - Copy the Client ID, open perch, press Ctrl+, and paste it in.

perch then shows a code to enter at twitch.tv/activate, and your follows list
appears. Nothing secret is ever stored in the binary. Sign out, at the foot
of Settings, forgets the sign-in; Sign in, where Sign out was, on Home, or
in any chat, starts a new one.

Without it: Home says "Not signed in" and offers you Settings,
and Popular, Categories and search say "Not signed in" too, since every
list perch asks Twitch for needs a sign-in, and so does a stopped pane's
offer of the channel's last broadcast. Watching a channel by name —
Ctrl+K and its name, or perch.exe followed by it — and its chat still
work.


Optional: the auth-token cookie
-------------------------------

Settings has a second field for the `auth-token` cookie from twitch.tv. It
unlocks Prime/Turbo ad suppression and subscriber-only qualities.

It is a full account credential. perch stores it in plain text and passes it
to streamlink on the command line, where any process running as you can read
it. That is a real tradeoff rather than an oversight, and everything except
those two features works without it. Leave it empty if you would rather.


Where things live
-----------------

  Settings   %APPDATA%\perch\settings.json
  Log        %LOCALAPPDATA%\perch\perch.log
  Images     %LOCALAPPDATA%\perch\images

Deleting the settings file resets everything, including the sign-in.


Getting around
--------------

  Space     Pause or resume          Ctrl+K     Command palette
  M         Mute or unmute           Ctrl+F     Search
  C         Show or hide chat        Ctrl+R     Refresh this list
  B         Show or hide the rail    Ctrl+,     Settings
  Up/Down   Volume                   Ctrl+0     Reset the pane sizes
  Ctrl+W    Close this pane          Esc        Back to browsing, or to watching
  Alt+Left  Back                     Alt+Right  Forward
  P         Pop out, or bring back   Z          Maximize, or show all panes
  Shift+Left/Right  Move this pane one place earlier, or later

Player keys act on the pane you last pointed at or clicked. While the cursor
is in a text box the keys stand aside for it, all but Ctrl+K, Ctrl+, and
Ctrl+R, which type nothing.

Point at a picture and its controls come up along the bottom of it: pause,
the speaker and the volume, then the quality, with two panes or more the
maximize, then chat, the guide, fullscreen and More, which with two panes
or more can silence every other pane (Only this one, then Hear all again),
pops the pane out, opens what it is playing on twitch.tv or copies a link
to it (a recording's link is at the moment it has reached). Those a key
also works name the key in their tooltips; the quality, the guide and More
have none. Alt and the mouse wheel over a picture change its volume (the
wheel alone does not), and a middle click mutes or unmutes it. While Twitch
plays an ad the picture holds still, and the pane's header says "ad break"
until it is over. A channel that is off offers its last broadcast, and
starts by itself when it comes back on if you follow it; a stream that ends
offers its recording from the start.

On a live stream a timeline runs above those controls, from the start
of the broadcast to now. Click or drag back along it and let go to watch
the broadcast's recording from that moment in the same pane, if the
channel keeps its past broadcasts; you need to be signed in. While the
stream is still on, LIVE at the end of the recording's seek bar takes the
pane back to the live stream.

P, or the icon beside a pane's ×, moves its picture into a small window of
its own that stays on top of other apps; its chat stays put. P again, or
Bring back, returns it. On the browse page P, or Pop out on the mini
player, does it for every pane there, each in its own window. Alt+F4 on the
small window brings the pane back; its Close closes the pane.

With more than one pane open, Z, or the maximize on its bar, gives the one
you are on the whole window, chat and all; the others keep playing, with
their sound, out of sight, and 1 to 4 or Tab hands the window to another.
Z or Esc shows them all again.

Drag a pane by its header onto another and the two swap places; Shift+Left
and Shift+Right swap the pane you are on with the one before or after it,
counting the way 1 to 4 do. Esc lets go of a drag. The order lasts until
Perch closes.

The icon with the two Ts, in a pane's header when it sits on chat, opens
the chat options: the text size, Small to Larger, and Time on every
message, which puts the time at the start of every message instead of once
a minute. They apply to every chat and are remembered.

Back and forward go through the pages you have been on, the way a browser
does. The mouse's side buttons and the arrows in the bar across the top do
the same. The house beside the arrows goes straight to Home from anywhere.

The guide icon, beside chat on a pane's controls, raises the guide over the
lower half of the watch page while every pane keeps playing: your live
follows, the rail's recommendations, Popular and Categories, as cards. A
pane whose stream is off or has ended has no controls, so its header has
the icon instead. Watch on a card (or a click on it) plays that channel in
place of the pane you opened the guide from; another pane's guide icon
makes that pane the one it replaces. + Add opens it beside the others. The
guide goes away after either, or with its ×, the guide icon of the pane it
was opened from, Esc, or a click outside it.

You can open up to four channels at once — click a card, or the + on a row in
the rail down the left. The bar across the top of the window holds the button
that folds the rail away, back and forward, the search box and Settings, on
either page. Rest the pointer on a live channel in the rail for a moment to
see its picture and title beside it.

Right-click a chat message to copy it, its sender's name, a link in it or the
name of the emote you clicked on.

Signed in, a live chat ends in a box that says "Send a message": click it,
type, and press Enter. Your message shows in chat when Twitch echoes it
back; if Twitch does not post it, its reason shows in that chat instead.
While the cursor is in the box the shortcuts stand aside, so M and Space
type (all but Ctrl+K, Ctrl+, and Ctrl+R, which type nothing). If the box
says "Sign in again to chat", your sign-in is from before perch could send:
press Sign in again beside it and enter the new code at twitch.tv/activate.
